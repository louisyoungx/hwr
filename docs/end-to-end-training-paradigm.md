# End-to-End Vision-Language-Bimanual Training Paradigm Without Experts (Historical)

> Status: Replaced on 2026-08-12 by the [Foundation Model Perception, World Model, and Imagination Reinforcement Learning Paradigm](foundation-world-model-training-paradigm.md)
>
> Decision date: 2026-08-10
>
> Scope: 3D simulation, local training, and future real-robot data and deployment

This document only explains the historical implementation and failure causes of P076–P080. It
no longer guides new development or training. Formal implementations must not continue from
the small vision front end, character-hash language encoding, direct model-free Actor-Critic,
or historical checkpoint/replay described here.

## 1. Conclusion

The platform no longer provides the Actor with action answers from hand-written experts,
rule-based trajectories, human demonstrations, or another teacher policy. The bimanual Actor
interacts directly with the environment and learns from the final task outcome:

```text
Procedural scenes and tasks ────────────────────┐
                                                v
Multi-camera data -> Visual preprocessing ──┐  Simulation/real-robot environment
Natural-language instruction -> Text encoding ──┼-> Bimanual Actor -> Safety layer -> Execute action
Robot proprioception ─────────────┘       ^                   |
                                    |                   v
                                    └── Reinforcement-learning update <- Outcome reward
                                             ^
                                             |
                                  Training-time privileged Critic
```

The Critic may read simulation ground truth during training to estimate action value, but it
does not produce actions, generate demonstrations, or get imitated by the Actor. Deployment
retains only vision preprocessing, text encoding, the Actor, and the independent safety layer.

This decision replaces the following legacy approaches:

- Hand-written waypoints, grasp poses, task stages, and rule-based experts;
- Behavior-cloning warm-up from rule-based experts or human teleoperation trajectories;
- DAgger correction labels generated by a teacher policy;
- Skill sequences such as “find, approach, grasp, and place” as training supervision;
- Extending single-arm scripts into purported bimanual training data.

Historical behavior-cloning and expert code may be used only to regress old experiments or
check the physics engine. It must not enter formal training data, initialization checkpoints,
reward computation, or evaluation action paths.

## 2. Human-Supervision Boundary

### 2.1 Humans Define Only Outcomes and Boundaries

For each task type, humans define only once:

- The range of initial states that may be sampled randomly;
- The natural-language task intent;
- Success and failure conditions computable automatically from environment state;
- Safety constraints such as collisions, joint limits, velocity, and torque;
- The distribution boundaries of the training and hidden evaluation sets.

For example, the success criterion for “put the plate and cup into the designated storage
location” checks only the container relationships and stability duration after the Episode
ends. It does not specify what the robot must pick up first, which hand to use, where to grasp,
or which path to follow.

### 2.2 Prohibited Human Labels

Formal training must not require humans to provide:

- Per-frame or per-segment action labels;
- Expert trajectories, corrective actions, or teleoperation demonstrations;
- Grasp points, end-effector waypoints, or base routes;
- Task stages, skill names, object tokens, or target tokens;
- Left/right arm role assignments;
- Scene-specific control state machines.

Rewards, success, termination, and safety events generated automatically by the simulator are
environment feedback, not action annotations. These fields are used by the Critic, replay
stratification, and audits, but must not enter the Actor input. The trainer must not require
scenes to provide an additional achieved goal, skill stage, or intermediate label from which
actions can be inferred.

## 3. Actor Runtime Contract

### 3.1 Visible Inputs

The Actor may receive only:

- Deterministically preprocessed head RGB-D, left- and right-wrist RGB, and validity information;
- The most recent several frames of visual history;
- A vector produced by a local text encoder from the raw natural-language command;
- Proprioception for the base, left and right arms, and left and right grippers;
- The most recently executed action;
- Quality information such as timestamps and dropped-frame masks that contains no task answer.

The following information must not enter the Actor:

- Ground-truth poses of simulated objects or targets, ground-truth contact, and success flags;
- Rewards, Critic features, task progress, and future actions;
- Script state, stages, skills, object IDs, or human plans;
- Any field available only in simulation and not reproducible on the real robot.

### 3.2 Joint Bimanual Output

At every control instant, the Actor jointly outputs a 16-dimensional base action:

```text
[base linear velocity, base angular velocity,
 left-arm-base-frame 6D end-effector twist, right-arm-base-frame 6D end-effector twist,
 left gripper target, right gripper target]
```

For each arm, the 6D components are normalized commands for end-effector linear velocity
`vx, vy, vz` followed by angular velocity `wx, wy, wz`. The runtime adapter converts them to
joint servo targets using damped least squares or an equivalent device-SDK controller. The
conversion uses only robot kinematics and the current joint state; it does not receive object
poses, grasp poses, task stages, or skill plans.

The hard deployment bounds are base linear velocity `±0.18 m/s`, angular velocity
`±0.50 rad/s`, and `±0.35` for each normalized twist component of both arms; the MuJoCo
adapter further maps these to a maximum linear velocity of `0.30 m/s` and angular velocity of
`1.0 rad/s`. Online exploration and Actor outputs must share these bounds so that the policy
cannot exploit Critic extrapolation errors to produce actions outside the training domain.

The network may predict an `H × 16` action chunk for several future steps at once. The arms
have no master-slave relationship. Runtime code must not fix one side, select the active hand
by scene code, or insert hand-switching logic outside the Actor.

The safety layer independently enforces joint, velocity, torque, collision, and action-validity
limits. It may reject an action, but it must not complete the task on behalf of the policy.

Before submitting an action, the simulation adapter checks severe collisions using a one-control
period physics copy. The real-robot adapter implements the same rejection semantics with joint
limits, torque/current, and proximity sensors. The barrier may replace a dangerous action with
a hold action, but it must not correct the action toward the task target. Replay and action
history must record the 16-dimensional action actually executed by the safety layer and count
interventions separately; a rejected Actor proposal must not be treated as environment
causality.

Training also uses a separate Safety Critic whose inputs are privileged state and the raw Actor
proposal; its supervision comes only from the deterministic safety barrier's “accept/reject”
result. It provides safety-cost gradients to the Actor, but does not enter the Actor input, is
not exported to the deployment model, and does not replace the runtime barrier. The reward
Critic continues to estimate environment causality only from executed actions, while the
Safety Critic estimates constraint risk only from proposals; the two must not be mixed.

## 4. Vision and Language Priors

### 4.1 Deterministic Vision Preprocessing

Each Episode retains raw frames, calibration parameters, and timestamps; policy inputs are
derived by a unified pipeline:

1. Synchronize the head and wrist cameras with robot state and generate missing-data masks;
2. Undistort using intrinsics and extrinsics, and align RGB with depth;
3. Clean invalid depth, isolated noise, and data outside the workspace;
4. Transform to a unified robot coordinate frame and construct a sparse point cloud or
   equivalent 3D representation;
5. Crop, resize, and normalize global and local views separately;
6. Assemble short-term history and mark duplicate frames, dropped frames, and unavailable cameras.

The preprocessing configuration is saved with the checkpoint. Training, closed-loop evaluation,
and real-robot deployment must load the same version.

### 4.2 Action-Label-Free Vision Learning

The vision front end is trained jointly with the Actor and may use self-supervised auxiliary
objectives from frames collected by the policy itself:

- Depth reconstruction and temporal consistency;
- Cross-view and cross-time contrastive learning;
- Occlusion recovery and camera-motion prediction;
- Auxiliary segmentation, depth, or optical-flow losses automatically output by simulation.

Auxiliary outputs do not constitute an object list or symbolic task plan. Simulation labels must
not flow back into the Actor input.

### 4.3 Natural-Language Prior

The language prior comes from a pretrained text encoder that runs locally, not a hand-written
command parser. The task generator samples environment goals and multiple natural expressions
at the same time; their grounding relationship is established only through the final Episode
reward, without an attached action sequence.

The text encoder is frozen initially; when data is sufficient, only upper layers are fine-tuned
or a low-rank adapter is used. Encoder weights, tokenizer, and hashes are registered with the
checkpoint, and training and deployment do not depend on an online service.

## 5. Expert-Free Reinforcement-Learning Loop

### 5.1 Direct Actor Training

The main algorithm is an asymmetric off-policy Actor-Critic. The first version centers on
continuous actions, maximum-entropy exploration, and experience replay. The Actor starts from
random policy parameters or action-label-free pretrained vision/language parameters; expert or
teacher checkpoint initialization is prohibited. Task intent is connected only through the
natural language, environment observations, and environment rewards visible to the Actor; the
training algorithm does not parse target objects or task structure.

The random Actor's final action layer uses small near-zero random weights and zero bias, placing
the initial mean actions of the base, arms, and grippers near neutral and giving positive and
negative exploration equal opportunity. This initialization reads no task, environment, or
action samples and encodes no pose or trajectory. The random-weight amplitude is written to the
manifest as a model configuration so reloads and training lineage remain auditable.

- The Actor reads only the deployable inputs in Section 3.1 and directly outputs bimanual actions;
- During training, the Critic may read full robot state, object poses, contacts, and environment goals;
- The Critic outputs only value estimates, not actions or intermediate plans;
- Rewards are computed only from task outcomes, safety constraints, time, and energy use;
- Neither Actor nor Critic may read human-defined task stages or scripted actions;
- Export removes the Critic, rewarder, and simulation ground truth completely.

Critic updates first scale environment rewards to a stable value range and apply conservative
Q regularization to random actions and current-Actor actions outside the replay distribution,
preventing the Actor from exploiting inflated values for actions not covered by replay. The
Actor optimizes the lower of the two Critic estimates rather than arbitrarily selecting one
value head, avoiding single-head estimation errors. This constraint only lowers value estimates
unsupported by physics data; it does not treat replay actions as supervision targets or
introduce a behavior-cloning loss.

The online policy uses a reparameterizable squashed Gaussian: the action network produces
state-conditioned means and the trainer maintains learnable per-dimension variances. Base and
bimanual samples are mapped to physical bounds with `tanh`, while gripper samples are mapped
to `[0, 1]` with `sigmoid`. Motion and grippers use separate variance bounds: motion retains
small continuous perturbations, while grippers cover nearly binary opening and closing and
hold one sampled value for several control steps to match finger-servo and contact-formation
timescales. The holder reads only the action stream and fixed duration, not the task or
environment. Critic bootstrapping and Actor updates both include transformed probability
densities and entropy regularization, so a visual state retains multiple feasible action modes
during training rather than relying only on a deterministic mean plus local noise. Deployment
exports only the action-mean network and uses deterministic inference; training variance,
entropy terms, and the Critic do not enter runtime.

Constraint learning uses two time scales: the reward Critic and Safety Critic update at `3e-4`
per training batch, while the Actor updates once every 10 Critic batches. The Actor uses
`3e-5` for the first 6500 Critic updates to learn frontier retention quickly, then drops to
`1e-5` to prevent an established spatial policy from drifting into high-amplitude, uncalibrated
action regions. Decay reads only the global optimization step, not task, contact, target, or
stage. Initial/final learning rates, decay point, and actual per-Episode learning rates are
written to training configuration, manifest, and records. The time-scale separation does not
require the Actor to reproduce replay actions and does not introduce an old-policy or teacher
consistency loss.

Physical servoing, contact formation, and safety rejection usually span multiple 20 Hz control
steps. Replay therefore stores fixed-window multi-step discounted returns, corresponding future
states, and the actual bootstrap discount, allowing the Critic to see the result caused by an
action over the subsequent physical window; the window is truncated immediately at Episode
termination. Multi-step returns only regroup rewards and states already produced by the
environment; they do not generate action targets or change the Actor's deployable inputs.

Formal training defaults to the full physical horizon defined by each task: 1200 control steps
(60 seconds) for the tray and storage basket, and 1600 control steps (80 seconds) for the
spring-loaded drawer. The command line may explicitly set a shorter limit for algorithm
diagnostics, but such runs must not be used for success rate, stable-hold, or single-arm
ablation acceptance, avoiding artificial return truncation before bimanual contact and transport
outcomes appear.

### 5.2 Automatic Task Distribution and Curriculum

The environment automatically samples room layouts, objects, containers, language expressions,
and physical parameters from configured distributions. A curriculum scheduler adjusts
difficulty from the sliding-window success rate without adding stage inputs to the Actor:

1. Start from a complete task distribution with short distances, little occlusion, and low noise;
2. Expand initial position, pose, and visual variation after success enters the target range;
3. Increase the easy-sample ratio when success declines, but never fall back to scripted control;
4. Continue mixing historical hard examples so the policy does not learn only the current level.

The curriculum changes the initial-state distribution; it does not decompose the task into
human-specified action steps.

The training interface has one hard boundary: policy actions may come only from random
exploration or the current RL Actor, and training code must not generate actions by scene. A
new scene may provide observations, action space, rewards, termination, environment-defined
success/safety outcomes, and legal environment transformations; it must not add a dedicated
training branch for a tray, basket, drawer, or other object. Success conditions and safety
constraints belong to the environment task interface and may read simulation ground truth, but
they must not return the next action, grasp pose, waypoint, or skill stage.

Automatic frontier stores only physical states actually visited by the current random or
learning Actor and ranks them using four task-agnostic signals: normalized Critic-state
novelty, current double-Critic TD error, per-state local reward-improvement speed, and the last
safe state before an environment-declared failure termination. Local reward-improvement speed
is computed from the current reward relative to that Episode's exponential historical reward
baseline; the improvement over an entire Episode must not be copied to every state. A failure
boundary is formed only when the environment returns `terminated=True` and the outcome is not
successful. A normal time limit and environment `truncated=True` are not failure boundaries,
and a timeout terminal state must not be promoted to the frontier automatically.

The four signals use tied-percentile normalization over the joint distribution of historical
and current-Episode candidates. Each Episode preferentially retains candidates dominated by
different signals, and capacity eviction retains at least every signal signature that has
appeared, preventing a single novelty signal or a cluster of adjacent terminal states from
filling the pool. A score signature indicates only which learning signal dominates; it does
not indicate left hand, right hand, contact type, or object class. States with nonzero safety
cost cannot enter the candidate set. The system does not read reach distance, target
coordinates, contact signatures, or hand-written geometric thresholds, and it does not
generate action answers.

Frontier snapshots store generalized position, velocity, acceleration, current actuator control
load, and the adapter's opaque solver state; these are dynamics states that have already
occurred, not future actions. After selection, the system performs one action-free state
restoration consistency check: it compares the backend fingerprint, generalized position, and
velocity, without closing grippers, moving arms, or running another probe policy. On failure it
returns to the original reset. The candidate pool, source Episode/step, learning signals,
selection count, and migration audit are saved with the checkpoint. Hidden evaluation and
final videos always use the original programmatic reset. Frontier entries produced by
hand-written distance/contact thresholds in old checkpoints are all discarded during migration
and do not participate in the new curriculum.

The reward adds a bilateral-neighborhood occupancy term over continuous outcome quantities:
`0.02 * exp(-max(d_left, d_right) / 0.08)`. It does not determine task stage, read object
class, or provide action direction. It merely gives a small positive signal when a policy
starting from an autonomous frontier has both arms nearby, rather than leaving immediately
because the potential difference is zero at reset. When either side is still 20 cm away, this
term is smaller than the per-step cost, so the policy cannot farm reward by stopping with one
arm or remaining far away; real bilateral contact, task outcomes, and safety constraints carry
greater weight.

Object-to-target displacement progress must not be scored under uncontrolled conditions. For the
tray and storage basket, target-distance reduction is accumulated only when both arms are in
simultaneous contact at two adjacent physical time steps; losing either contact rolls back the
current continuous progress used for reward. Success also requires the object to have moved
from its initial Episode distance into the target tolerance during one continuous bimanual
contact segment. This physical-causality evidence may remain after release, but it is revoked
immediately if the object leaves the target tolerance. The policy cannot obtain terminal reward
by “touching with both arms first, then pushing into place with one arm” or by moving an object
away after controlled placement and pushing it back. Drawer opening likewise accumulates and
forms completion evidence only during continuous left-arm contact with the drawer handle;
object displacement still requires each side to maintain its corresponding physical contact.
Current progress is part of the training-time Critic state. Historical maxima and completion
latches are used only for environment success checks and audits, and neither enters the Actor
observation. This constraint judges only continuous physical causality; it generates no grasp
pose, task stage, or action label.

Physical quantities such as reach distance, contact steps, and synchronized-contact windows
may still be written to per-Episode diagnostics to help humans determine whether the robot is
actually doing the task, but they must not affect frontier admission, task sampling, replay
stratification, or Actor updates.

Multi-task collection allocates sampling budget using the same task-agnostic signals: higher
state novelty, TD error, reward-improvement stagnation, and failure-boundary strength increase a
task's probability of subsequent collection. Every task also retains fixed probability lower and
upper bounds; for the current three tasks, no single task may exceed `0.55` by default.
Different units are first converted to tied ranks across tasks and then combined. Exactly equal
metrics must receive the same priority; task-ID ordering must not break ties. Temperature and
probability caps are generic scheduling parameters, so adding a new scene requires no new
distance scale or training branch. Task sampling, frontier selection, and action exploration
use independent random streams. The scheduler outputs no actions and exposes neither task
stages nor sampling scores to the Actor.

### 5.3 Autonomous Replay and Failure Utilization

Replay stores only raw transitions produced after the current random exploration or current RL
Actor actually executes them in the physical environment. Failed Episodes enter the traceable
replay buffer unchanged, with reuse increased through a separate failed-return quota. The main
training path does not perform hindsight goal relabeling or synthesize goals, rewards,
termination, or actions, and does not require new scenes to provide a goal vector. Hindsight
rows used only by the Critic and pre-expanded augmentation copies in old checkpoints have no
meaning under the new storage semantics; forks must explicitly discard old replay and retain an
auditable discard record.

Replay first uses task-isolated capacity and balanced sampling, then reserves independent quotas for
failed returns, the highest-ranked novelty samples from normalized state changes, samples with
positive and highest-ranked local reward-improvement speed within an Episode, and proposals
actually rejected by the safety layer. Reward improvement is the increase in the current-step
environment reward relative to the historical exponential baseline; remaining on the same high
reward plateau does not continue to occupy the improvement stratum. Stratification uses only
transitions, environment rewards, and safety costs; it does not read object class, reach
distance, contact type, or target geometry. All classifications affect only sampling and Critic
updates, do not enter the Actor input, and do not produce action supervision.

The simulation environment declares only transformation IDs legal for the current transition.
Replay does not store transformed copies; at sampling time, a fixed task-agnostic probability
synchronously transforms observations, training-time Critic state, and executed actions. Thus
one real transition occupies only one primary capacity slot. The algorithm knows only generic
transformation groups, not trays, baskets, drawers, or other objects.

The main exploration for continuous actions comes from the Actor's own maximum-entropy
distribution. Cold-start random exploration and low-probability global bursts may still use the
structural prior of left/right robot symmetry: some end-effector perturbations are sampled in
pairs by reflection across the x-z plane, some gripper openings and closings are sampled in
pairs, and the remaining samples keep the sides independent. The sampler must not read cameras,
language, object state, environment goals, task IDs, or stages, and it must not provide an action
that should be executed. It only changes the unlabeled exploration distribution so that
bimanual concurrency no longer depends on two independent random processes happening to hit the
same state simultaneously.

Physical grippers need real servo and contact-convergence time between issuing a close command
and forming stable contact between the jaws and an object. Action exploration therefore adds
“actuator-settling” samples: base and arm velocities are set to zero for a fixed window, while
the two gripper commands are held open or closed according to an explicit Bernoulli probability.
Grippers are randomly sampled as a pair or independently under the same left/right symmetry
prior. In addition to low per-step settling probability, settling may begin at the first
control step of an Actor Episode with a fixed probability, preventing the policy from disturbing
a rare physical pose it has autonomously discovered. This process reads no observation, task,
goal, contact, reward, or stage, and does not use object state to decide when to close; it only
lets replay cover both sustained motion and stationary-convergence actuator dynamics. The
per-step probability, first-step probability, window length, closing probability, zero-motion
constraint, and gripper pairing mode must be written to the action-exploration audit and fully
disabled during evaluation.

The platform allows a new run to be created from a checkpoint that has passed hash and
no-expert-lineage checks. An ordinary fork may change only total Episode count, the
capacity-bounded replay limit, task-agnostic action exploration, frontier-reset mixing
probability, task-sampling temperature/probability cap, replay ratios, and generic unlabeled
consistency weight. Replay compatible with Actor, Critic, and storage semantics may be
inherited. If replay storage semantics change, the fork must use explicit `--discard-replay` to
discard only old replay and record the reason and count for each partition in lineage. Old
frontier and task-sampling history incompatible with the current curriculum schema must also be
explicitly discarded and counted; results based on old distance thresholds, timeout boundaries,
or Episode-level state labels must not be presented as discoveries from the new curriculum.

If a task's scene assets, initial geometric relationships, or dynamics distribution changes
substantially, old transitions and physical snapshots no longer match the new environment
distribution and must not be mixed in through an ordinary fork. An audited fork must instead be
created with explicit `--reset-task TASK_ID`: discard only that task's ordinary/failed/novelty/
physical-progress/safety replay, frontier snapshots, task-sampling history, and randomization
curriculum window. State for other tasks and shared Actor/Critic parameters continues to be
inherited, while parent Episodes are retained only as historical lineage. Lineage must record
the discarded task, replay-stratum counts, frontier count, sampling history, curriculum state,
and the reason for the distribution change. Task reset generates no actions, stages, or labels
and must not be used to selectively delete failures.

The maximum-entropy objective is separated by actuator type: the temperature is `0.02` for
continuous base and bimanual motion and `0.002` for the two grippers; gripper initial log-std is
`-0.5`, with range `[-3.0, 0.2]`. Grippers still explore random opening and closing, but they
do not maintain a latent standard deviation near or above 1.0 for long merely to match the
entropy of 14-dimensional continuous motion, which would disrupt the closing window near a
handle. This setting does not specify when to close or read contact or task stage; the closing
mean is still learned by Actor-Critic from outcome rewards.

The Actor's 16-dimensional joint-action means and variances use the same base learning rate;
gripper gradients and parameter groups receive no extra multiplier. The Critic's local ranking
of gripper opening and closing still changes early on; amplifying gripper updates would make
the Actor drift too quickly before value rankings are calibrated, indirectly damaging bimanual
spatial control through real closed loops and action history. Training therefore learns opening
and closing only through outcome Q, the lower gripper entropy temperature, and correct actuator
regularization bounds, without task-, contact-, or stage-triggered learning-rate shortcuts. The
unified learning rate is locked by unit tests.

Action-magnitude and step-change regularization applies only to the 14 continuous base and
bimanual motion dimensions, not to the two gripper positions. Gripper opening and closing
naturally require switching from one position to another; including them in a continuous
velocity slew penalty would systematically pull the policy toward fully open after reset,
offsetting the Critic's closing value. Physical gripper smoothing is provided by a fixed
12-control-step task-agnostic holder that only continues the Actor's output and does not decide
when to open or close. Motion and gripper regularization bounds are locked by unit tests.

In addition to local correlated noise, training retains low-probability, short-duration random
bursts across the full action space to prevent replay from remaining permanently confined to one
local neighborhood after the Actor reaches an action boundary. A burst still uses only action
bounds, duration, and left/right symmetry reflection; it reads no task or environment field. The
runtime safety layer continues to screen every proposal, and executed actions and rejection
events enter their respective replay fields.

### 5.4 Preventing Single-Arm Collapse

Reserving bimanual dimensions in the action vector is not enough. The task distribution and
acceptance criteria must make bimanual cooperation indispensable:

- The simulation environment declares legal transformations, such as lateral reflection, through
  the unified runtime contract. The generic augmentation library synchronously processes
  cameras, proprioception, Critic state, and actions by transformation ID; the trainer does not
  know “tray,” “basket,” or “drawer.” Undeclared transformations are never applied. The same
  Actor computes both the original and transformed observations and directly enforces
  `Actor(T(o)) = T(Actor(o))`; both sides contribute gradients, with no EMA Actor, teacher
  action, or human action label;
- Randomly exchange the side on which objects and handles appear to remove a fixed-dominant-hand shortcut;
- Add wide-tray and dual-handle-container tasks that require maintaining two contact points simultaneously;
- Add concurrent tasks in which one hand holds a spring-loaded drawer while the other places an object;
- Record valid left/right arm contact, object-hold duration, and concurrent-operation windows, but do not directly reward “having moved both hands”;
- Lock the left arm and right arm separately during evaluation to verify that bimanual tasks cannot be bypassed by one arm.

Ordinary tasks may let the policy determine whether one arm is sufficient; the platform does
not force both hands to make useless movements for superficial symmetry.

### 5.5 Closed-Loop Data Flow

```text
Complete/frontier reset -> Actor exploration -> Environment reward/termination -> Autonomous replay -> Actor-Critic update
       ^               |                                      |
       |               ├-> Failure/novelty/safety transition tiers ----|
       |               └-> Visited physical frontier states ------------┘
       └--------------- Task-agnostic curriculum and hard-example resampling
```

Behavior-cloning loss, expert action error, and teacher consistency loss are not formal
training metrics.

## 6. Local Training and Reproducibility

All training must run on the existing local machine without a remote inference API. The trainer
may automatically select MPS, CUDA, or CPU and must:

- Use parallel environments, experience replay, a low-resolution start, and mixed precision to control compute;
- Save task distributions, preprocessing configuration, text encoder, model configuration, random seeds, and code commit;
- Periodically save the Actor, Critic, optimizer, temperature parameters, curriculum state, and replay buffer for interrupted-resume training;
- Export a deployment checkpoint without the Critic or privileged fields;
- Reload the deployment checkpoint from disk and run closed-loop evaluation;
- Isolate training and evaluation by Episode, layout, object instance, language expression, and random seed.

## 7. Current Implementation Order

1. Freeze the 16-dimensional bimanual action, ActorObservation, environment reward, and termination contracts;
2. Connect joint control of the left/right arms, left/right grippers, and base in MuJoCo, and remove runtime paths that fix the arm on the storage side;
3. Implement programmatic task sampling, terminal success criteria, safety penalties, and legal environment-transformation declarations;
4. Implement the expert-free asymmetric Actor-Critic, autonomous replay, and checkpoint resumption;
5. Train the bimanual Actor directly with low visual randomization first, then automatically expand layout and visual difficulty;
6. Add left/right mirroring and tasks that physically require bimanual cooperation, then complete single-arm ablations;
7. Run closed-loop evaluation on at least three household scenes, unseen layouts, and unseen language expressions;
8. Generate unedited videos, input audits, training lineage, and ablation reports.

Legacy expert debugging no longer blocks this order and cannot serve as completion evidence for any stage.

The local closed-loop acceptance command (after training completes) is:

```bash
hwr-evaluate-bimanual-rl runs/bimanual-rl/<run-id> \
  --seed-count 20 --video-seed-count 1
```

By default, this command runs three evaluation conditions for each of the three scenes:
normal, left-arm locked, and right-arm locked. Third-person, head, left-wrist, and right-wrist
videos are synchronously written frame by control frame by the same evaluation process, with no
editing or frame interpolation. `report.json`, `acceptance.json`, and `manifest.json` store
per-Episode results, fixed thresholds, the training manifest hash, Actor hash, unseen seeds,
and video hashes.

## 8. Stage Acceptance

A training stage is complete only when all of the following conditions are met:

- Training lineage proves that no expert data, teleoperation actions, teacher checkpoint, or action labels were loaded;
- Automated tests prove that Actor inputs contain no privileged fields or human-defined stage/skill fields;
- The same raw observation produces deterministic, consistent vision preprocessing under the same configuration;
- Every policy action contains base, left-arm, right-arm, left-gripper, and right-gripper outputs;
- The deployment checkpoint runs in a process without the Critic, rewarder, or task-control scripts;
- The checkpoint is reloaded from disk and executes closed-loop actions on isolated seeds;
- Bimanual-required tasks meet the success threshold, and locking either arm prevents meeting the same threshold;
- Videos, Episodes, model hashes, code commit, and configuration are mutually traceable.

Lower historical behavior-cloning loss, successful expert scripts, reproducible training seeds,
or movement by both arms do not constitute evidence that training is complete.
